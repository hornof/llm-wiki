## What It Is

Chinese AI lab, one of the canonical **Chinese open-weights frontier vendors** the wiki tracks alongside [[z-ai|Z.ai]], [[qwen|Qwen]] (Alibaba), and [[moonshot-ai|Moonshot AI]]. Known for the **DeepSeek-V3** and **DeepSeek-R1** open-weight model lines, which established the "credible open-weights peer at a fraction of frontier price" pattern that now anchors the [[ai-margin-collapse|AI-margin-collapse]] thesis.

A fresh datapoint for the [[ai-margin-collapse]] / open-weight-parity thread (efficiency + price undercutting the frontier), landing the same week [[kimi-k3|Kimi K3]]'s open-weight parity was confirmed. *(Efficiency claims concrete on benchmarks but unverified on real workloads.)*

Also named the same week as one of three labs running [[anthropic|distillation campaigns against Anthropic]] ([[ai-margin-collapse]]) — the architecture story and the attribution story are running in parallel and should not be conflated. **Eval details are missing and both briefs say verify-before-repeating** — architecture claims unconfirmed.
- **2026-10-17 — V4.1-Flash-1017 rolled out across website, app and API** ([[dailybrief-roundup-2026-10-17]], Digg). DeepSeek says it improves benchmarks, task speed and output length over its predecessor. **A dated point release of the 09-12 model, not a new architecture**, and the date-suffix naming suggests DeepSeek is now shipping V4.1 on a rolling cadence. *(Vendor claims via Digg summary; no benchmark figures captured.)*
- **2026-10-19 — DHS order to remove DeepSeek from federal civilian systems made public** ([[dhs-deepseek-federal-removal-2026-10-19]], WSJ via Digg). It bars future purchases and covers affiliates and **third-party products built on DeepSeek.** RuntimeWire dates the order **2026-07-24** and says a 60-day exception window had already closed. *(**Contested 2026-10-20:** TechCrunch gives the order date as 10-20, the Federal Register publication date, and says agencies can request exceptions. The Federal Register record (document 2026-21447) gives no signing date in its metadata. If 07-24 is right, the order ran nearly three months before it was public; that reading is not settled.)* *(**Settled 2026-10-21 from the order itself.** The signed order, printed as an appendix to Federal Register 2026-21447, is headed *"Exclusion and Removal Order #2026-01 … Original Release Date: July 24, 2026"* and is signed by DHS Secretary Markwayne Mullin. Agencies had to exclude and remove DeepSeek *"immediately after issuance"*, and exception requests were due *"within 60 days of the issuance of this order"*, so the window closed around 2026-09-22. **RuntimeWire's 07-24 and its closed exception window are right. TechCrunch's 10-20 is the publication date, and its "can request exceptions" doesn't match the order's own deadline.** The order was in force for agencies for nearly three months before it appeared in the Federal Register. Whether it was reported publicly before 10-19 is unknown; the wiki found nothing earlier.)* **The third-party clause is the part that reaches practitioners:** a vendor selling a fine-tune or a product on DeepSeek weights is caught too, which makes the model's origin a procurement question for anyone selling to federal agencies. → [[frontier-ai-governance]] *(Summary of WSJ and RuntimeWire; order text not read.)*
- **2026-10-20 — the DHS order is published in the Federal Register; Beijing objects** ([[dailybrief-roundup-2026-10-20]], TechCrunch; Federal Register 2026-21447). The notice confirms the scope: DeepSeek and its affiliates excluded from federal civilian procurement, and removal of DeepSeek, affiliate and **third-party products "built using DeepSeek implementations."** DHS's stated reason is that US government users' data could be accessible to Chinese authorities under Chinese law. China's Foreign Ministry (Mao Ning) opposes *"overstretching the concept of national security."* TechCrunch adds context: a December 2025 law already barred DeepSeek from intelligence and national-security systems, and a **September CISA report accused DeepSeek and five other Chinese labs, Alibaba and Moonshot among them, of large-scale distillation.** The wiki had not recorded the CISA report. *(No DeepSeek response reported; removal deadline not stated.)*
- **2026-10-20 — DeepInfer: the production inference stack, open-sourced in part** ([[deepseek-deepinfer-release-2026-10-20]], GitHub, Apache 2.0). It supports V4.1-Flash only, on **NVIDIA SM100 and Huawei Ascend 950**. Not released: the KV cache system, prefill/decode disaggregation and data-parallel load balancing, which the README says a full service needs. **Ascend support as a first-class target is the notable part**, the same day the US order bars DeepSeek from federal systems: the stack is built to run off NVIDIA too. *(Performance figures appear only in chart images; not checked.)*
  > **2026-10-21: the production numbers behind it** ([[latent-space-deepseek-inference-economics-2026-10-21]], Latent Space AINews). DeepSeek's engineering report claims **155T tokens/day** from one cluster (cache hits included), **$38 revenue per accelerator-hour** off-peak, a 96% input-cache hit rate and 400 ms average time to first token. Most analyst estimates of the margin fall between 74% and 87%. vLLM matched DeepInfer's GB200 decode throughput within 6%, which it calls decode parity only. **The withheld parts are the ones those numbers depend on:** a 96% cache hit rate is a KV-cache result, and KV-cache management is what wasn't released. → [[ai-margin-collapse]] *(First-party, unaudited. DeepSeek didn't say which hardware earned the revenue.)*
