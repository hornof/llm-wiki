## Who They Are

Ryan Greenblatt is a lead AI-safety researcher at **Redwood Research** (an AI-control / alignment lab), known for work on **AI control** (making powerful-but-possibly-misaligned models safe to *use* via oversight + containment, rather than assuming alignment) and for a widely-noted ARC-AGI result. Surfaced into the wiki as a recurring high-signal voice on **recursive self-improvement (RSI)** and what happens when *AI automates AI research itself* — the capability-scaling question the wiki tracks via [[frontier-ai-governance]] and Jack Clark's RSI thread.

## Their Current Focus

- **2026-08-12/13 — "What happens once AI can automate AI research?"** ([[dwarkesh-patel|Dwarkesh]] long-form, [[dailybrief-roundup-2026-08-13]]): Greenblatt on **recursive self-improvement**. The reframe worth tracking: the load-bearing question isn't "superintelligence" per se but **whether the bottleneck shifts from compute to AI labor** — if AI R&D can be automated, whoever *owns the deployment loop* moves faster than the field (and its safety practices) can adapt. Pairs with Anton Korinek's RSI economic-growth modeling ([[jack-clark|Clark]]'s in-house thread) and the [[frontier-ai-governance|"pace AI"]] coordination question. His **AI-control** framing is the practical counterpart to the containment lessons in [[reward-hacking]] (box the model; assume it may be misaligned).

## Notable Takes

- **AI control over pure alignment**: safety should not assume we can make models *want* the right thing — it should make even a possibly-misaligned model *safe to deploy* through oversight, monitoring, and containment. The applied-safety register directly relevant to the [[reward-hacking]] / [[frontier-ai-governance]] threads.
