## Who They Are

Dylan Patel is the founder and chief analyst of **SemiAnalysis**, the most-cited independent research shop on semiconductors, datacenter buildout, and AI compute economics. His beat is the **physical/capital substrate under the AI industry** — GPUs, fabs, power, datacenter real estate, and the unit economics of training vs. inference. Where [[jack-clark]] tracks the policy/capability frontier and [[simon-willison]] tracks the practitioner/tooling layer, Patel is the wiki's **compute-and-capital-supply-side** voice: when he makes a structural claim about who owns compute, it moves partnership and positioning strategy. *(Distinct from [[dwarkesh-patel|Dwarkesh Patel]], the podcaster — the two are frequently co-cited and Dwarkesh has interviewed Dylan; do not conflate.)*

## Notable Takes

- **The moat is infrastructure, not models** — as inference commoditizes, durable advantage accrues to whoever owns compute + power + datacenter real estate, not to model quality per se. The through-line across both the neocloud and compute-consolidation framings.
- **Open weights raises the capital bar rather than lowering it** — commoditized weights mean you compete on scale of serving infrastructure, which *increases* the capital required to be a frontier player.
- **Convergence usually precedes a cull** — mass movement into a single business shape (neoclouds) rhymes with the search-engine wave; most converge, few survive.
