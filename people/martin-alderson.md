## Who They Are

**Martin Alderson** is an independent analyst who writes on **AI infrastructure economics and unit costs** at martinalderson.com. Wiki-tracked as the **coiner of the [[ai-margin-collapse|AI margin collapse]] thesis** — the sharpest external articulation of how open-weights inference pricing threatens frontier-lab margins. Promoted to an entity page at the 2026-09-10 lint (2 dedicated source pages + anchor of a concept page).

## Their Current Focus

- **2026-07-06: "GLM 5.2 and the coming AI margin collapse (Part 1)"** ([[martin-alderson-glm-5-2-ai-margin-collapse-2026-07-06]]): the argument that frontier labs amortize expensive training across **~90%-gross-margin inference**, and that a credible open-weights peer ([[glm-5-2|GLM 5.2]] at ~$4.40/MTok vs Opus ~$25/MTok, <20% of retail, near-zero switching cost) collapses that inference P&L industry-wide. The anchor for [[ai-margin-collapse]].
- **2026-06-08: "xAI is looking more like a datacentre REIT than a frontier lab"** ([[alderson-xai-datacentre-reit-pivot-2026-06-08]]): argues [[xai|xAI]]'s datacenter-rental pivot reframes frontier labs from research-orgs into **landlord/REIT economics** — *"frontier labs don't fund themselves; REITs do."* First wiki-captured frontier-lab-as-REIT framing; pairs with the [[dylan-patel|compute-consolidation]] + [[saas-disruption-thesis|"Land, Power, Shell"]] threads.

## Notable Takes

- **"AI margin collapse"** — inference margin, not training, is where labs make money, and good-enough open weights commoditize it. The unit-economics lens the wiki uses to evaluate any applied-AI business.
- **Frontier-lab-as-REIT** — at scale the durable business is compute/real-estate landlord economics, not model research.
