## Who They Are

Pieter Levels (**@levelsio** on X) is a Dutch indie hacker and **solo operator** who runs several profitable businesses alone — Nomad List, Remote OK, PhotoAI among them — with no employees and no outside capital. He is the wiki's canonical **indie-hacker register**: a builder whose positions come from shipping alone at speed, and who is structurally hostile to the VC-backed-startup frame.

Paged 2026-09-13. An entity page was explicitly deferred on [[dailybrief-roundup-2026-05-23|2026-05-23]] *"pending a third tracked-source mention"*; that bar was passed some time ago (five-plus surfaces since May) and the [[levelsio-harness-startups-software-is-dead-2026-09-13|2026-09-13 "software is mostly dead"]] thread — his first sustained argument rather than an observation — made the gap conspicuous. He is also cited as a live example inside [[naval-ravikant-saas-is-next]] and [[forbes-mehra-solo-founder-playbook-2026-08-03]], so the wiki was already leaning on him as evidence without a page to hold him.

## Their Current Focus

Solo operation at the limit of what one person plus agents can run. As of 2026-05, **he had not written code in six months** — delegating to coding agents while staying the operator. In 2026-09 his attention is on what the AI platform shift does to the economics of founding a software company at all, and his answer is bearish.

## Notable Takes

- **"Software is mostly dead and hardware it is" (2026-09-13)** ([[levelsio-harness-startups-software-is-dead-2026-09-13]]): reacting to [[goodhartproof-yc-demo-day-domain-specific-harness-2026-09-11|jessy's YC Demo Day post]] — *"@ycombinator's batch literally only has harness startups or hardware startups… it's debatable if anyone will actually need a custom harness or it won't just be generically offered by the AI frontier companies."* The **strongest statement of the platform-absorption objection** the wiki holds against [[domain-specific-harness]]. He endorses Henno's mechanism outright (*"That's it exactly"*): *"hard for any harness to outperform a frontier team over time, especially if frontier teams have infinite compute on models that aren't publicly available."*
- **Harnesses are features, not startups**: *"Like ChatGPT for Accountants or Claude Medical."* Agreeing with the *"system tools vs OS maker 15 years ago"* analogy.
- **This cycle breaks the desktop→SaaS precedent** — his most falsifiable claim. Told that harnesses are just the new form factor the way SaaS was, he concedes the category and rejects the outcome: *"Their models are big enough to do niches well. That's why it's completely different now than before."* Prior platform shifts left room for application companies because the platform could not serve niches; he argues frontier models can. **Testable, and the whole [[domain-specific-harness]] thesis turns on it.**
- **"Skills features in AI coding agents are overrated"** ([[dailybrief-roundup-2026-05-23]], 2026-05-23): *"equivalent to basic text files,"* preferring direct instructions on tasks and methods. Austen Allred's counter — *"skills enable reuse across sessions"* — is the wiki-canonical position; see [[skill-md]]. The disagreement is itself the signal: Levels is the indie-hacker register, Allred the educator register, and how the [[skill-md|Skills]] primitive lands differs by cohort.
- **Has not written code in six months** (2026-05-23): the **operator-not-coder** pattern at indie-hacker scale, alongside [[sairahul1|sairahul1's 13-agent playbook]] and the [[agentic-engineering|outcome-validation-over-line-review]] shift.
- **Claude Code `/effort` daily-reset bug** ([[dailybrief-roundup-2026-05-24]], 2026-05-24): a practitioner bug report on the CLI, captured as tooling-reliability signal.
- **EU sub-€150 customs duty warning** ([[dailybrief-roundup-2026-07-01]], 2026-07-01): operator-side commerce friction, from the hardware/physical-goods side of his portfolio — relevant given his 2026-09 pivot argument toward hardware.

## How to Read Him

He has a **standing anti-VC prior**, and *"VC-backed software startups are largely dead"* is a conclusion he is disposed to reach. He is also the wiki's most-cited live proof that a solo operator can run multiple profitable software businesses — which cuts against his own *"software is dead"* framing. Weight his mechanisms; discount his finality.
