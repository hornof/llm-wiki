## Summary

[[greg-isenberg|Greg Isenberg]] solo, 26 minutes, published on or about **2026-09-25** (Apple lists it as ~3 days before 2026-09-28; exact date not shown). On [[meta|Meta]] opening **Muse connectors** to developers, argued as an app-store-era parallel, with four startup ideas attached.

## Key Claims / Takeaways

- Meta is **opening Muse's connectors to third-party developers**.
- Isenberg reads this as an **app-store moment** — a platform opening a surface that then gets built on by outsiders.
- Four startup ideas named: **lead generation for business suppliers**, **home-repair dispatch**, **paddle/court matching**, **family dinner planning**.

## Judgment

**The app-store analogy is doing a lot of work and the description does not defend it.** What made the App Store a moment was distribution plus billing plus a review process, not an open integration surface. Connectors without a payment rail and a discovery surface are closer to a plugin directory — which the industry has now shipped several times, mostly without the outcome.

**But it lands against something the wiki already tracks.** Muse opening connectors while the agent-access question is unsettled is the platform choosing the permissive side of a live argument — the one where retailers block agent traffic rather than serve it. **Two large platforms are moving in opposite directions on whether third-party agents may reach into a business's surface**, and this episode is one of them, from an interested commentator rather than from Meta.

**The four startup ideas are idea-list content, not evidence.** Recorded because Isenberg's idea lists have occasionally preceded real category formation, not because any of these is signal.
