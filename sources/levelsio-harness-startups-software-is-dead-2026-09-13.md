## Summary

[[pieter-levels|Pieter Levels]] quote-posts [[goodhartproof-yc-demo-day-domain-specific-harness-2026-09-11|jessy's YC Demo Day observation]] two days later and turns it into the **bear case**: if a whole YC batch is building domain-specific harnesses, and harnesses get absorbed into the frontier labs' general offering, then *"software is mostly dead and hardware it is."* This is the **platform-absorption objection** — logged as unanswered when [[domain-specific-harness]] was filed on 2026-09-12 — now stated forcefully by a named operator, with a mechanism and a historical analogy, and corroborated by several repliers.

## Key Claims / Takeaways

- **The observation, harder than jessy's**: *"I just saw this @ycombinator's batch literally only has harness startups or hardware startups. So the proof is in the pudding. In the most early adopting tech pioneering place that's Silicon Valley new startups aren't even building software anymore."*
- **The objection**: *"it's debatable if anyone will actually need a custom harness or it won't just be generically offered by the AI frontier companies."*
- **The conclusion**: *"So software is mostly dead and hardware it is."*
- **The mechanism, from a replier Levels endorses outright** — Henno (@henno_4e): *"It's gonna be pretty hard for any harness to outperform a frontier team over time, especially if frontier teams have infinite compute on models that aren't publicly available."* Levels: *"That's it exactly."* **This is the sharpest version of the absorption argument in the wiki**: the asymmetry is not distribution or capital, it is that frontier teams hold *unreleased models plus unlimited compute*, so any harness built on the public API is optimizing against a moving target it cannot see.
- **The historical analogy** — Chainfire (@ChainfireXDA): *"Every new software startup seems to be yet-another-harness, but for domain X. If successful, the big players will just include and kill them next cycle. It's like system tools vs OS maker 15 years ago."* Levels: *"Yes they sound like features not startups. Like ChatGPT for Accountants or Claude Medical."*
- **The form-factor counter** — Marc Köhlbrugge (@marckohlbrugge): *"Harnesses are still software. But just like software moved from Desktop to SaaS in 2000s, it's a new form factor harnessing (pun intended) the latest tech to make the best possible product."* Levels concedes the category but not the outcome: *"Yes just doubtful… Their models are big enough to do niches well. That's why it's completely different now than before."* **His claim is specifically that this cycle breaks the desktop→SaaS precedent** — previous platform shifts left room for application companies because the platform could not serve niches; he argues frontier models can.
- **The escalation** — David Galbraith (@daveg): *"The only software in current YC cohort is domain specific harnesses, but maybe even harnesses are general purpose. In which case, folks, VC backed software startups are largely dead."*
- **The hardware qualifier** — Jon Yongfook: *"even a lot of the hardware is essentially some meat space wrapper around a harness."* Undercuts Levels's own escape hatch.
- **Dissent** — Gee (@gagan_deep7): *"nope software is not dead yet. in fact, if i were a new entrant I'd double down on software… natural language is just not there yet."* And Cam (@camhart73) names the unresolved bridge: the benefits still have to reach non-technical users, and whether the mobile gatekeepers (Apple/Google) will *allow* LLMs to build that bridge is undecided.
