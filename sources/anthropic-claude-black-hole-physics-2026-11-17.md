## Summary

Anthropic research post, dated 2026-11-17. Agents running Claude, set up by an Anthropic researcher as a side project, worked for about six weeks on 22 candidate problems and solved two: whether the Hawking-Page phase transition stays abrupt in the regime where spacetime has no definite shape, and the exact limit of the simplest version of the modular bootstrap ceiling. This is Anthropic describing its own model's work; the preprints are linked from the post but were not read, and the brief flags that physicists had not yet responded.

## Key Claims / Takeaways

- **Setup:** researcher Hongbin Chen asked Claude for open problems on black holes and the bootstrap method; it returned *"22 candidates"*. Separate agents took each one, could read each other's progress posts, and *"ran in the background, with no special setup, for about six weeks."*
- **Result 1, Hawking-Page:** a calculation set out in 2003 and never finished. Claude *"did it by running a small research group of copies of itself"* and found the transition is abrupt, so the same sudden switch appears at both extremes. A physicist who had separately used Claude on the same problem reached the same answer by a different method, weeks later.
- **Result 2, modular bootstrap:** one agent spotted a pattern in numerical results; another, working on sphere packing, turned it into a method that reproduced *"a sphere-packing result recently proved by an OpenAI model"*. An earlier Claude agent had failed *"in 61 parallel attempts"*. Half the final proof follows the OpenAI proof's strategy.
- **Checking:** *"In September, the results were formally verified in Lean"*, and *"a physicist checked every main result."* Claude wrote the paper *"with minimal guidance"*.
- **Scope, in Anthropic's words:** *"Neither result describes our own universe directly, and neither has practical applications"*.
