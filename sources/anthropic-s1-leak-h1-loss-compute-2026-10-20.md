## Summary

TechCrunch "In Brief" item by Rebecca Bellan (2026-10-20), working from an Anthropic S-1 that was leaked to The Wall Street Journal. The filing reports an **$80.7 billion net loss in the first half of 2026 on $16.3 billion in revenue**. It also says AWS, Google and Microsoft increasingly treat Anthropic as a competitor, which makes cloud capacity harder to get. Read with the Digg page that collects WSJ, Bloomberg and reporter posts on the same filing (https://digg.com/tech/hxa3xssc).

## Key Claims / Takeaways

- **H1 2026: $80.7B net loss on $16.3B revenue** (TechCrunch, citing the WSJ). The Information's Anissa Gardizy, quoted on the Digg page, ties the loss to *"accounting adjustments related to previous financings."* So it is not a measure of cash burn, and the size of the adjustment is not given.
- **Compute commitments: three figures, not one.**
  - WSJ (via Digg): *"around $450 billion in cloud computing agreements over seven to ten years, the bulk of them noncancelable."*
  - Gardizy: *">$600 billion on compute in the coming years, inclusive of cloud agreements and chip leases."*
  - WSJ and TechCrunch: Anthropic *"could"* spend more than $750 billion, some of it still being negotiated. Reuters and the FT reported this figure in late September.
- **Suppliers:** AWS, Google and Microsoft, plus data centres Anthropic leases itself and third-party operators. The filing worries those operators have a *"limited operating history."*
- **Customer concentration:** two customers make up about a fifth of sales. They are not named.
- **Liability:** the filing warns Anthropic could owe damages for harmful AI behaviour beyond its insurance cover.
- **Q3:** TechCrunch restates the $16.8B third-quarter revenue figure as **up 45% from the previous quarter.**
- **Filing status:** TechCrunch calls it an S-1 *"leaked to"* the WSJ. That reads as a document not yet public on 2026-10-20. See the note on [[anthropic]].

**Two brief draft claims not used:**
- *"Betting the entire AI scaling thesis on a single vendor lock-in."* The filing names three hyperscalers plus self-leased and third-party capacity.
- *"$80.7B net loss on $450B in noncancelable cloud commitments is … a structural problem."* The loss includes financing-related accounting adjustments of unknown size, so it can't be read as an operating loss.
