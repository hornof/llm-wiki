## Summary

[[gergely-orosz|Gergely Orosz]] (The Pragmatic Engineer) posts an observation (2026-07-27) that **CTO / Head of Eng / VPE folks at startups and mid-sized companies are leaving or burning out** — hiring for these roles is hard, and even filled roles often churn within months into career breaks or founder/fractional paths. Ankur Tyagi (@TheAnkurTyagi) quote-tweets in agreement: engineers *"no longer chasing VP Engineering / CTO titles… building agencies… becoming consultants… raising capital & building companies."* Anchor for [[engineering-leadership-ai-era]]. *(Practitioner observation, small-n — Orosz notes it's from talking with 3–4 people, mostly SF/NYC.)*

## Key Claims / Takeaways

Orosz's eight reasons the eng-leadership role is churning:
1. The role *"is just a sh*t one to do right now"* — AI creates **unrealistic expectations**.
2. The leader realizes **the company has no strategy to win with AI**.
3. They realize they **won't get the AI-native experience** they need to stay employable long-term, and the setup won't allow it → *"career suicide"* to stay.
4. Their predecessor left for the same reasons (churn begets churn).
5. The genuinely good ones in SF/NYC can do **fractional CTO** work instead — and do. (Good fractional CTOs get *"massive pulls to go fulltime… most refuse."*)
6. The role now demands reading the business/room and picking the *right* company — *"very important now. Also very hard."*
7. Starting your own thing as a strong eng leader is **easier than before** (they can raise funding) — many build on the side.
8. Some are simply **burnt out**, and now is a fine time for a months-long break.

**Comment-thread corroboration**: *"a fundamentally mispriced role — CTOs do most of the technical work and take the onus… but get a fraction of the CEO upside"* (Conor Myhrvold); *"impossible to plan long-term strategy while expectations are higher than ever"* (Tom Watkins); *"opportunity cost is immense… so a lot of folks start their own company"* (Nenad Mancevic).

## Why it matters

- **Directly germane to the wiki owner** (a former VP Eng / CTO returning to hands-on AI): names the exact structural squeeze on the role the owner is navigating — and the alternative paths (fractional, founder, hands-on IC) being taken instead.
- A high-signal read from the field's most-cited engineering-management voice, corroborated by a second practitioner and a rich reply thread.
