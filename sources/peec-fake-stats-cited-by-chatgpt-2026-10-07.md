## Summary

Metehan Yesilyurt of Peec AI, a generative-engine-optimisation (GEO) vendor, dated 2026-10-07. He generated 50,000 statistics with GPT-4.1 in a day for a new domain, says *"Every one of them was fake,"* and never checked them. ChatGPT then fetched and cited the site at answer time. The figures spread to an academic paper, a bank's investment research and other publications. A second test used real, checked statistics on Peec's own site.

## Key Claims / Takeaways

- **Scale (self-reported):** more than 600,000 bot requests in 30 days, about 72,000 of them from ChatGPT-User; more than 10,000 ChatGPT referral sessions since March; 330+ referring domains (Ahrefs/Semrush), including Shopify, DHL and Bluehost.
- **Ranking:** second for visibility on a tracked prompt set, behind Statista.
- **Google** deindexed or stopped surfacing the site; ChatGPT did not.
- **Mechanism:** retrieval at answer time, not training data. The author speculates about later crawls but shows no evidence. The brief's "LLM training pipelines are citation-laundering" framing goes further than the post.
- **The site now carries a disclaimer.** No indication the downstream citers have corrected anything.
- **Caveats:** a marketing post for a GEO vendor, describing a deliberate experiment in polluting the web. All figures are the author's own analytics.
