## Summary

Simon Willison's write-up of his AI Engineer Code Summit keynote, posted on the first anniversary of Claude Opus 4.5. He names three points in the past year where coding models changed what he could do: Opus 4.5 (November 2025) made Claude Code reliable enough to use daily; Claude Fable 5 (June 2026) introduced "Fable class" models that pursue a goal on their own; and Opus 5.5 (September 2026) was the first model that seemed to understand what makes a game fun. He's undecided whether Fable 5.2 is a fourth.

## Key Claims / Takeaways

- **Opus 4.5 made Claude Code a daily tool** (Willison): *"Opus 4.5 was the first model I had used with Claude Code where the default result was generally the right thing."* GPT-5.1's Codex models did the same for Codex that month.
- **"Fable class"** (Willison): his one-line definition is *"Give it a goal, give it the right tools, and watch it cook."* He counts *"Claude Fable 5, 5.1, 5.2 and Claude Opus 5.5"* (Opus 5.5 is *“cheap Fable”*), plus GPT-6 Astra, and maybe Gemini 4 Neon. *"These now represent the baseline for what I expect from a frontier model."*
- **Example** (Willison): with five WebAudio microphones, Opus 5.5 and then Fable 5.2 located a mystery beep *"to within about 40cm."*
- **Models learned 3D in four months** (Willison): they *"got really good at 3D modeling"*, starting with Fable 5 and accelerating with Opus 5.5, GPT-5.6 Sol, GPT-6 Astra and Gemini 4.
- **Opus 5.5 and fun** (Willison): told *“This game isn’t fun enough yet. Make it a LOT more fun.”*, it turned his RTS game around *"after Opus spent 12 hours playtesting new features and rebalancing the gameplay."* *"Opus 5.5 was the first model I’d used that appeared to understand fun."*
- **Fable 5.2, maybe a fourth** (Willison): *"if you can imagine and then clearly describe a piece of software, Fable can build it."* Evidence offered: three Datasette alphas and five LLM releases *"all in the last three weeks"*.
- **Bottom line** (Willison): *"I don’t think I’ll ever go back to typing most of my code by hand."*
