## Summary

[[jack-clark|Jack Clark]]'s newsletter, issue 480 (fetched 2026-11-11). Its lead item: Physical Superintelligence (PSI), an AI-science startup, says its system proved a quantum-computing conjecture open since 2008, using a non-public [[anthropic|Anthropic]] model. Other items: Meta's Tactus glove for recording hand-manipulation data, a 2016 NASA study on bootstrapping industry on the Moon, and Core Automation's training run across two distant clusters.

## Key Claims / Takeaways

- **PSI proves the Aaronson–Ambainis conjecture (PSI's claim, quoted by Clark).** A system *"running a non-public Anthropic model, found a proof for it"*. *"The system had a complete proof of the conjecture during the third day of the run, and Lean, a proof assistant in which a computer checks every step, accepted it."* It then *"improved the result twice"*. PSI's post wasn't fetched. Which Anthropic model was used is not stated.
- **Clark's frame:** *"I believe we’re in the early stages of the singularity"*, the line from his IMF speech ([[import-ai-477-clark-imf-singularity-2026-10-19]]).
- **Core Automation:** *"a mixture-of-experts model with roughly 32B total parameters, 32x sparsity was trained across two clusters about 9,000 miles apart"*, on different accelerators (GB300 and B300). Their argument: *"The biggest bottleneck in deployment of accelerated compute is not the chips themselves but power, grid equipment and land"*.
- **Meta's Tactus glove:** *"a textile-integrated tactile and pose sensing glove designed from the ground up for naturalistic manipulation data collection"*. That is robot-training data, not a product.
- **Lunar industry (NASA, 2016):** *"In the baseline case with maximum manufacturing rate, a total of 41 MT is launched to the Moon."*
