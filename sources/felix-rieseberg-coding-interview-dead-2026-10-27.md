## Summary

Felix Rieseberg leads engineering for Claude.ai, Cowork and Claude Code Desktop at Anthropic. In this post (2026-10-27), with an [X thread](https://x.com/felixrieseberg/status/2115200995546566910), Rieseberg says they removed the coding interview from their hiring loops this month because it no longer predicts who succeeds. Their advice to junior engineers, or anyone without status, is to build a working prototype of one ambitious idea instead of waiting for approval. **It's one manager's hiring practice, not an Anthropic-wide policy.** They also say they haven't yet worked out what should replace the interview.

## Key Claims / Takeaways

- **The decision:** *"This month, I laid the traditional coding interview to rest."* On X: *"The coding interview is dead, at least for my team at Anthropic."*
- **Why:** *"There’s no more point in giving you a little React challenge than there is in quizzing people about assembly; Claude is now better at both."* And: *"Our most impactful builders are successful regardless of how good they are at these tests."*
- **The claim about juniors:** *"I’m seeing engineers who are comparatively new fly past people who are probably better coders and have been around much longer."*
- **Why prototypes now win:** organizations used to make you earn status before backing your idea, because building was costly. *"Execution isn’t expensive anymore."*
- **Five steps:** pick business impact over backlog; pick a radical idea; build it over a weekend; focus on one idea per prototype; use your outsider's view of the product.
- **Claude Code's desktop app as the example:** two relatively new engineers built a prototype over a weekend, and *"The first public version shipped roughly eight days later."*
- **No replacement yet:** *"I haven't quite gotten there yet"*. For now they read resumes and portfolios for small prototypes that show taste.
- **Replies:** one candidate says Anthropic gave them a take-home last week. Others ask how experienced engineers will be assessed, and one says most companies will still run coding interviews in seven years.
