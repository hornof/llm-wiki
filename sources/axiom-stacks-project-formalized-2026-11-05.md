## Summary

TechCrunch reports that Axiom Math used a fleet of AI agents to formally verify the Stacks Project, a large open reference work in algebraic geometry, in Lean. Axiom pitches verified mathematics as a foundation for AI-written software and science. The article frames it against a flood of unverified AI math claims.

## Key Claims / Takeaways

- **Scale.** The agents translate *"roughly 7,654 pages into about 16 million lines of computer-checked code"*.
- **Why this library.** Axiom's Ashvin Swaminathan: *"When you look at what has been formalized in mathematics, there's this huge black hole. And that black hole covers algebraic geometry"*.
- **How the agents were organized.** A partial hierarchy: *"The agents were finding tasks for themselves and identified things that they could work on in parallel."* Axiom also *"trained its own models to be good at math"*.
- **The context.** *"Last month, OpenAI alone released 719 manuscripts claiming solutions to some of the field's hardest problems."*
- **Company.** Valued at $1.6B (*"The $1.6B startup"*); *"raised a $200 million Series A led by Menlo Ventures"* in March 2026. CEO Carina Hong.
- **Not established:** how the 16 million lines were checked for fidelity to the original text, beyond passing Lean. A proof can check while formalizing the wrong statement.
