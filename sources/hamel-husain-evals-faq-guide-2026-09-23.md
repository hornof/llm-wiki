## Summary

[[hamel-husain|Hamel Husain]] publishes a consolidated **AI Evals FAQ**, drawn from **60+ hours of office hours** from the evals course he and **Shreya Shankar** have taught to **~5,000 engineers and PMs**. He calls it *"the most valuable free resource we've created on AI Evals (not exaggerating!)."*

Captured as a **reference resource**, not a claim. It is the practitioner-side counterpart to a gap the wiki has been circling all month: [[developer-productivity-measurement]] records that September's agentic-coding claims supplied Speed abundantly and Quality not at all — and evals are the discipline that would supply the missing half.

## Key Claims / Takeaways

- **Format is the point.** *"It allows you to find answers to your eval problems w/o searching aimlessly. Humans: pick the row that sounds like you. Agents: point it at the post."* A reference indexed by **problem** rather than by concept — and explicitly authored to be consumed by an agent as well as a person, which is [[skill-md|the skills pattern]] applied to documentation.
- **Provenance is the credential**: 60+ hours of recorded office hours, ~5,000 students, with **15 FAQs added in the last two weeks** and recent additions badged. It is maintained, not a one-off post.
- Co-taught with **Shreya Shankar** (@sh_reya).

## Why it matters here

The wiki holds a lot of *assertions* about agent reliability — [[loop-engineering|verifier discipline]], evidence-driven testing, confidence thresholds, change failure rate — and comparatively little on **how to actually construct an eval**. This is the most credentialed practitioner index of that craft the wiki has captured, and it is the natural companion to the *"design the verifier, not the prompt"* thread.
