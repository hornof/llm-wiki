## Summary

A services-side operator argues that the **billable-day model is structurally incompatible with AI-native delivery**, and offers a test for telling real AI-native firms from repackaged ones. Directly relevant to [[ai-native-service-companies]], which the wiki defines via Gustaf Alstromer's YC RFS as *selling the outcome of a service done rather than the tool used to do it* — this is a practitioner claiming to run that model.

**Heavily promotional** (the post ends in a discovery-call booking link), and the numbers are unaudited self-report. The *mechanism* is the durable part.

## Key Claims / Takeaways

- **The structural conflict, stated plainly**: *"A consultancy has to keep an expensive person busy. $350 an hour, billed by the day. The work is built to last. Scope expands. The team expands. Phase two gets sold in month three."* Efficiency gains cannot be passed to the client without shrinking the firm's own revenue — so they aren't.
- **The inversion he claims**: *"Every engagement we run at @LimestoneHQ has fewer people on it now than when it started. The firm is growing faster than it ever has."* Growth decoupled from headcount-on-account is the whole thesis; if true it is the AI-native services shape, and if false it is marketing.
- **A usable diagnostic** — the most portable thing here: *"When a mid-sized or large engineering shop says they've gone AI-native, I ask one question: how many people have you let go, and why are you still hiring more? If the answer is none and lots, they aren't passing any efficiency to you."* And: *"If your vendor's team on your account has grown since AI arrived, you're paying for their business model, not yours."*
- **The delivery unit**: an *"AI velocity pod"* — **one senior engineer, agents across the whole SDLC, and a fractional architect. Roughly 1.5 FTE where a five-person team used to sit."* Claimed: **98% of shipped code isn't handwritten.**
- **Harness vocabulary in a services context**: *"engagements shrink: the work gets done, the harness takes more of the load, and we pull people off."* — [[domain-specific-harness|the harness]] named as the thing absorbing billable hours.
- **What he concedes consultants are still worth**: *"A dynamic consultant at $5K an hour who unblocks the thing that's been strangling operations for two years is worth every dollar."* The target is **generic** advice, not expertise: *"In 2026, if the advice isn't tied to a real problem, you can open a model, ask for the top five strategy frameworks… and start Monday."*
- **What consultancies actually sell**, in his reading: *"cover: I hired a top firm, so I made the responsible choice."* For a mid-market company, *"an expensive way to avoid deciding."*
- He also flags the new failure mode on his own side: firms that *"assemble a team to ride the AI demand wave, hire people with case studies, and suddenly the firm has case studies. But there's no history behind the logos."*
