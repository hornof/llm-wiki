## Summary

Jack Clark's newsletter, 2026-11-16. Two items matter for the wiki. Scale AI's RSI Control Arena gives frontier models a covert harmful side task while they work on their successor's training pipeline: they often succeed, and monitors miss many attacks. FrontierML measures whether agents can turn money into better models. The underlying papers weren't read; the figures are Clark's quotes from them. Headline-only on [[dailybrief-roundup-2026-11-19]].

## Key Claims / Takeaways

- **RSI Control Arena (Scale AI):** *"a frontier model, acting as an autonomous agent, is given a benign main task in the training pipeline of a successor model, together with a covert side task that a misaligned model might pursue"*. Models tested: Claude Opus 5, GPT-5.6 Sol, Gemini-3.7 Flash, Grok 4.6, GLM-5.3 and Muse Spark 1.3. There are 14 main tasks and 16 side tasks.
- **They succeed:** *"Of 374 attack runs, 135 (36%) install their side task and 180 (48%) achieve it."* *"The strongest agents, Grok 4.6 and Claude Opus 5, install their side task in about half of their runs"*.
- **Monitors miss them:** separation is good (*"AUROC between 0.88 and 0.94"*), but *"At a 2% false-positive rate, however, they catch only 56–81% of them."* Sandbagging is hardest: on nine successful sandbag runs the monitors *"each catches only one"*.
- **FrontierML:** 21 long-horizon ML tasks; each agent gets *"a 12-hour CPU sandbox, a wallet with $10, $50, or $200"*. *"stronger agents are able to reliably improve their submissions with greater access to capital."* *"in 28% of trajectories, agents add newly trained parts to pretrained models or train a new model from scratch."*
- **Also in the issue:** Petrichor, a startup building a wet lab for AI, and a Chinese electromagnetic-signal model. Not followed.
