## Summary

TechCrunch (2026-11-11) on CoreWeave's third-quarter results. The neocloud is changing how it pays for its buildout: customer prepayments, loans tied to specific contracts, and short-term deals at higher prices, in place of unsecured debt. Revenue is growing fast and the company is still losing money. This item came from the first ai-pulse run on 2026-11-11, which the second run replaced.

## Key Claims / Takeaways

- **Results:** *"CoreWeave reported $3.66 billion in revenue, up 168% from the year prior, but it also posted a $709 million net loss and $846 million in quarterly interest expense."*
- **No unsecured debt:** CEO Michael Intrator: *"We will not use the unsecured debt market to fund our business at current yields, nor do we need to"*. Its backlog is *"$132.5 billion"*.
- **Customers pay up front:** it expects *"about $9 billion in customer prepayments and deposits over the next three months"*. TechCrunch notes Nebius and IREN have relied on large Microsoft prepayments too.
- **Prices rising:** about 25% in July and another 10% later in the quarter.
- **Short-term capacity sells at about twice the long-term price.** Intrator: *"selling capacity at more than $40 million per megawatt annually, while pricing for new infrastructure on four- and five-year contracts is moving to $20 million to $25 million per megawatt"*.
- **The bond market disagrees:** *"Last month, yields on some of the company’s bonds hit above 15%"*.
- **Why it matters here:** compute is scarce enough that a neocloud can charge more for short contracts, while lenders still price its debt as risky. Both are true at once. [Judgment.]
