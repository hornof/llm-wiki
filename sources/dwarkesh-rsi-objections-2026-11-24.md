## Summary

Dwarkesh Patel and his general manager Max Farrens go through published objections to Patel's 2026-11-13 essay ([[dwarkesh-rsi-superintelligence-2026-11-13]]), which argued for superintelligence by 2030. Patel restates his dates, concedes several points (the essay leans on intuition pumps; METR's finding is one data point; compute growth limits progress if automation doesn't arrive), and argues that partial automation of AI research still speeds it up a lot. Transcript with timestamps.

## Key Claims / Takeaways

- **His dates, restated** (00:11–00:12): *"my median year for fully automating the best AI researchers is 2028, and then superintelligence the year after, so something like 2029."* He puts *"like 25% chance"* on superintelligence next year. Later: *"I don’t think we’ll see the intelligence explosion next year. I expect that will happen from 2028 to 2029"*.
- **Concedes the evidence is thin** (00:44): METR found Anthropic's rate of progress on well-scoped tasks *"is currently two times what it was a year ago"*, and *"I agree that you can’t just extrapolate that one data point."* Also concedes that rising token spend (OpenAI's top researchers now at *"$23,000 worth of tokens a day"*, per Patel) may not mean more progress.
- **Data, not architecture** (Patel's own study with a Princeton student): comparing 2019–2026 corpora and architectures, *"data explains more of the progress than the architectures"*, and the gains came from filtering methods ML researchers can build without domain experts. That's his reply to "AI can't automate making data."
- **Partial automation still compounds** (Patel, on Agustin Lebron's objection that any human in the loop means no recursion): *"your uplift or your speed-up as a human that is doing a job is hyperbolic with the fraction of tasks that are automated"*, citing Amdahl's law.
- **Compute is the real limit** (Henry Dowling's objection, quoting Epoch): *"the global compute supply is growing only 2.6x annually"* while labs need about 4x a year. Patel: *"he’s totally right"*; if research isn't automated by 2030, progress slows.
- **Many AIs checking each other** (on Krier's objection): Patel agrees independently trained models monitoring each other would help, and worries a fast takeoff produces *"a single heritage of AIs that are all basically clones of each other"*.
- **Near term** (Patel): by *"the end of next year"* (2027) he expects AI *"you actually can describe as a competent colleague who is 24/7 working for you"*, owning whole projects.
