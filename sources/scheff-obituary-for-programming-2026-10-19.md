## Summary

[[simon-willison|Willison]] (2026-10-19) links Jeremy Scheff's essay at dumbmatter.com, which I read only through Willison's quotes. Scheff's argument: programming paid well because employers needed people to solve hard problems they couldn't solve themselves, and coding agents erode that. Willison's reply is that the loss depends on what you enjoyed. For people who mainly want to build working systems, LLMs are a gain; for people who loved solving the puzzles themselves, *"the loss is very real."*

## Key Claims / Takeaways

- **Scheff:** *"You got paid to work on tricky little logic puzzles."* *"The trickier the puzzle, the more you got paid."* *"We set the strategy, we made the timeline, we defined what was and was not possible."*
- **Willison:** for those who value building systems over the problems along the way, *"LLMs are the gift that keeps on giving."* *"If directly solving those problems was a big part of your enjoyment, the loss is very real."*
- **Not used:** the brief's draft (*"the puzzle wasn't the job — it was the permission structure"*) adds a thesis neither author states.
