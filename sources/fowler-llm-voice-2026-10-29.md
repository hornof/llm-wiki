## Summary

A short bliki post by [[martin-fowler]] (2026-10-29) on the "uncanny valley" reaction readers have to LLM-written prose. Fowler advises authors not to have an LLM write a draft, even one they then edit, because the voice is hard to remove. Writing is how they think, so handing it over means handing over the thinking. They make exceptions for disability, translation and documents no human will read.

## Key Claims / Takeaways

- **The reaction is human, not technical:** *"it's not the em-dashes, it's the load-bearing tapestries"*. Detectors miss the point, since *"By the time I'd reach for a tool to tell me what I'm reading, it's already too late"*.
- **No LLM drafts:** *"authors should not fall into the trap of getting the LLM to write a draft."* Even accepting rephrasing suggestions is risky. An outline may be fine, *"but only as far as an outline."*
- **Writing is thinking:** *"As more people delegate writing to LLMs they will also delegate thinking and comprehension to LLMs."*
- **Exceptions:** Jim Highsmith, who uses an LLM because of Parkinson's disease; colleagues writing in a second language; form letters and reports *"I suspect no-one will ever read"*.
- **Site policy:** authors who use AI declare how, and Fowler states: *"I did not use AI or LLMs in any way while writing this post."*
- **Relevance to the owner:** the owner's anti-AI style guide targets the same tells. Fowler's point is that rules for wording help less than not starting from an LLM draft.
