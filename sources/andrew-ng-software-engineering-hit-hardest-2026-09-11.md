## Summary

An r/siliconvalley post (2026-09-11) relaying a clip of [[andrew-ng|Andrew Ng]] naming **software engineering** as the profession AI is hitting hardest — while noting that job openings in it are **still going up**. The resolution he offers: *"threatened"* was never a population-level question, it is a **workflow-level** one. The second half of the clip is aimed at university curricula and their structural inability to keep pace.

## Key Claims / Takeaways

- **AI eats 30–40% of the software-engineering role**, in Ng's framing. Not the whole job, and not zero.
- **Openings are rising anyway** — the population-level and workflow-level questions have different answers, and conflating them produces the wrong prediction in both directions.
- **The split is behavioural, not competitive**: *"The engineers still busiest didn't out-compete the model on that slice; they stopped fighting for it and built the 60–70% it can't touch instead."* The ones falling behind are *"still writing code like it's 2022, before ChatGPT."*
- **There is no signal telling you which side you are on** — *"there's no single moment that tells you which side of that line you're on."* That absence is the actual risk being named, and it is the part the headline misses.
- **The institutional-lag argument**: a curriculum committee, then a faculty senate vote, then a year or two before a new course exists — against a job market reshaping every few months. Ng's framing per the post: *"That lag isn't a flaw in the system. It's the system working as designed, just aimed at a problem that no longer moves at its speed."*
