## Summary

TechCrunch (Aditya Mehta, 2026-10-26) on a SpaceX regulatory filing: an Apollo-managed entity will buy about $40B of GPUs and server hardware and lease them to SpaceX. It's a lease, not a loan, so SpaceX gets the chips without borrowing for them. The same day, Anthropic's S-1 disclosed up to $120.5B of committed spend with SpaceXAI ([[anthropic-s1-public-filing-2026-10-26]]), which shows who the capacity is partly for.

## Key Claims / Takeaways

- **Size:** *"roughly $40 billion in financing for AI chips and related data center equipment"*. SpaceX calls it the *"largest committed GPU financing"*.
- **Structure:** Apollo's entity *"will buy the chips and related server hardware, then lease the equipment to SpaceX"*. The leases run *"about five to five-and-a-quarter years"* from delivery of each tranche.
- **Timing:** close expected by year-end; leases start *"in the first half of 2027"*. Apollo says the hardware is Nvidia's *"next-generation Vera Rubin chips"*.
- **Payback claim:** the CFO, Bret Johnsen, says SpaceX sees *"less than one-year payback periods"* on its AI infrastructure. That's a company statement with no method given.
- **Context:** Q3 AI segment revenue *"$7.6 billion of SpaceX’s $14.6 billion in revenue"*; Q3 capex *"nearly $20 billion, up from $6.9 billion a year earlier"*; Q4 guidance *"$35 billion to $40 billion in the fourth quarter alone"*.
- **Musk on neoclouds:** *"That means the 200+ neoclouds are going to see guppy-level death and consolidation"* (on X). His argument is that debt markets have closed to GPU renters without other revenue. That's a competitor talking, not evidence.
