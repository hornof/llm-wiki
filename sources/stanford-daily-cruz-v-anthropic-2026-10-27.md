## Summary

The Stanford Daily (April Yu, 2026-10-27) on Stanford faculty whose books were in the pirated library at issue in *Bartz v. Anthropic*, and on two who opted out of the settlement to sue separately. **The headline describes the 2025 Bartz ruling, not a new one.** The article's own lede says a judge ruled "in a May 2026 lawsuit", which mixes up the two cases. Judge Alsup's ruling came in *Bartz*. The May 2026 filing is a separate suit, *Cruz v. Anthropic*, which hasn't been decided.

## Key Claims / Takeaways

- **Bartz, already in the wiki** ([[anthropic-copyright-settlement-1-5b-approved-2026-07-20]]): Alsup held that training on lawfully acquired books was fair use, and that keeping pirated copies in a central library was not. The settlement was *"$1.5 billion"* for *"nearly 500,000 registered works"*.
- **Cruz v. Anthropic (new to the wiki):** Tobias Wolff and Chang-rae Lee opted out and filed it in *"May 2026"*. It is a non-class suit seeking a jury trial and *"up to $150,000 per infringed work"*, against *"the approximate $3,000 of damages per work"* in Bartz. *"Wolff and 27 other authors say the settlement isn’t enough."*
- **What they want beyond money:** *"a permanent injunction against Claude model training using their works"*.
- **Paul Goldstein (Stanford Law, a Bartz class member):** licensing every work is impractical because of *"transaction costs"*.
- **Industry context:** *"over 70 infringement lawsuits against AI companies"*. The Authors Guild is pushing Congress for a licensing requirement.
