## Summary

Google's two Argon posts, fetched on 2026-10-28 to fill a gap: the wiki had recorded Argon only as a headline. The [first post](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/gemini-models/gemini-4-argon/) (2026-09-30) announced Argon for cyber defenders in the Fairwind Program. The second (2026-10-13) made it generally available through the API, AI Studio, Antigravity, the Gemini app and AI Mode in Search. **This settles the wiki's dating.** Argon was announced on 09-30 and released on 10-13, which is why the Carbon post says Argon *"was launched two weeks ago"*. All figures are Google's.

## Key Claims / Takeaways

- **Context:** *"an industry-leading 1M unified input-output token limit via the Gemini API"*. Output is now 1M tokens, *"up from the previous 64K tokens"*.
- **Benchmarks Google cites:**
  - *"DeepSWE v1.1 (77.9%)"*
  - #1 on the Vals Index (*"ranking #1 on the Vals Index"*)
  - Zapier's AutomationBench, where it *"ranks #1 with a score of 51.3%"*
  - *"LVBench"* for long video at *"91.7%"*
  - CWE-bench v1 with *"a top score of 68%"*
- **Internal use:**
  - In Antigravity, agentic code submissions rose *"by more than 35% while reducing code rollbacks by nearly a third"*.
  - A Chrome refactor involved a *"~6 million line legacy folder"* inside Chromium's 40M-line codebase.
  - Fleet memory optimizations freed *"over 300 TiB of memory"*.
  - C/C++-to-Rust migrations reached *"800K+ lines for the Fuchsia Zircon kernel"*.
- **Safety:** shortcut attempts on coding benchmarks fell *"to just 2%"*. Prompt-injection success on Gray Swan IPI was *"0.7%"*.
- **Price:** *"$2 per million input tokens and $10 per million output tokens"* until *"November 30, 2026"*, then $4/$20.
