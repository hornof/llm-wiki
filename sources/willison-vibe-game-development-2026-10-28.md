## Summary

Simon Willison (2026-10-28) says October 2026 is an inflection point for vibe-coding computer games. They built four browser games with Claude Opus 5.5 without reading the code, and they enjoy playing them. In their view the change is game design: the model now makes games that are fun, not just games that run. They ran one game's prompts through GPT-6 Pro for comparison and found the result played worse. **It's one experienced user's informal comparison, not a benchmark**, but it's the first hands-on Opus 5.5 assessment from a named, independent practitioner the wiki holds.

## Key Claims / Takeaways

- **The claim:** *"I think we’ve reached a new inflection point in October 2026, and this time it’s for vibe coding computer games."* They compare it to November 2025 for coding agents.
- **What improved in Opus 5.5:** *"3D modeling"*, music generation, and *"Game design (!)"*. The test prompt was *"Make it a LOT more fun"*, which worked.
- **Vendor comparison:** *"GPT-6 Astra isn’t quite there yet. It can absolutely build a game, but its sense of fun doesn’t match that of Opus 5.5."* The GPT-6 Pro version of the RPG *"looks great, but I don’t think it plays nearly as well."*
- **Four games, all HTML and JavaScript in the browser:**
  - Tree Appreciator was one-shot in plain Claude chat.
  - Emberwick is an RPG with a story of about 15 minutes (*"takes about 15 minutes to play through"*).
  - Battle Drones is an RTS with 2–4 player multiplayer, released under Apache 2.0 and already forked.
  - The fourth is a pumpkin-farming game.
- **Tip:** *"telling models to use three.js works really well."*
- **Where the work goes now:** *"Building games that play well is shockingly easy now. The new challenge is coming up with concepts"*.
- **Model-vs-model play:** Battle Drones has a mode where models play each other; they show Claude Haiku 5.5 against GPT-6 Luna.
