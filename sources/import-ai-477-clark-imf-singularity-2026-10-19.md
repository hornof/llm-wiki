## Summary

[[jack-clark|Jack Clark]] reprints his Per Jacobsson lecture, given at the IMF meetings in Bangkok on 2026-10-17. His claim is that AI has started advancing science at the frontier, sometimes on its own, and that this is the start of the singularity. He defines that, after Vernor Vinge, as AI speeding up science and technology faster than people can keep up. The talk ends with a list of policy asks.

## Key Claims / Takeaways

- **Software:** AI matches or beats most software engineers. Anthropic's researchers produce almost ten times as much code as in the company's first four years.
- **Maths:** AI helped propose solutions on Navier–Stokes, the Jacobian Conjecture and several open Erdős problems. ("Helped propose" is Clark's wording; no claim of accepted proofs.)
- **Biology:** viruses designed from scratch; AI-found drug candidates, some in trials.
- **Benchmarks:**
  - Humanity's Last Exam: from 8% at launch (2025-01) to 67.7%, set by [[claude-opus-5-5|Claude Opus 5.5]].
  - FrontierMath: from 2% (2024-11) to 94%.
  - TheoPhysEval: [[gpt-6-astra|GPT-6-Astra]] at 98% (49/50), after experts found errors in their own answer key while checking where the model disagreed with it.
- **Economics:** Anthropic modelling of singularity-like conditions projects US GDP of 44 trillion by 2030 (currency not stated in the summary).
- **Policy asks:** build power, data centres and supply chains; fund physical labs and robotics so discoveries can be tested; shorten lab-to-clinic and lab-to-factory paths; let raw-material-supplying countries build science capacity; strict human oversight in biology; rules for how much oversight AI-run AI research needs; decide which experiments humans should run and how much of AI-created science humans must understand; pace development.
- Clark says parts were ad-libbed and aren't in the text.
