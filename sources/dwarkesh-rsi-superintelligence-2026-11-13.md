## Summary

[[dwarkesh-patel|Dwarkesh Patel]]'s essay (2026-11-13) explaining why he moved, in about three months, from "at least the better part of a decade" to a median of 2028 for AI that matches the best human AI researchers, and AI at least as good as the best human experts in every field by 2030. The argument runs through math: AI got good at well-scoped, verifiable math problems very fast, and he argues AI research is more like that kind of work than like inventing new theory. He then models what automated researchers do to the pace of progress. Only the main body was read here, not the technical appendix.

## Key Claims / Takeaways

- **The forecast:** *"If I had to pick my median year for when automated researchers will be as good as the very best human AI researchers (and assuming the labs don’t intentionally slow down), I’d guess 2028."* He puts *"a 25% chance that it doesn’t happen until after 2032"* and says it could come in 2027.
- **Evidence he cites:** METR's finding that Anthropic's progress rate on well-scoped tasks is *"2x what it was a year ago"* (see [[recursive-self-improvement]]); OpenAI's heaviest researchers going from about $1,000 to *"over $7,000 a day"* of internal coding agents; Anthropic's report that Claude *"leads” more than 26% of Anthropic’s AI R&D work"*.
- **The core analogy:** ML research rewards *"persistently and intelligently matching dozens of different combinations of ideas"*, which is what AIs have shown in math. His example is the transformer, assembled from pieces already in the literature.
- **Why it speeds up:** automated researchers would be faster, as good as the top researchers, and far more numerous. He estimates *"around 80% of the algorithmic progress at a given frontier lab is driven by its top 50 researchers."* Compute for experiments stays a bottleneck.
- **Two outcomes:** a *"software-only singularity"* if AIs improve faster than ideas get harder to find, or a *"spark"*: *"something like three to six years of AI progress in a single year"*, then a slowdown until more compute arrives.
- **The limit he names:** RL works on tasks that can be graded inside a datacenter; building a business or winning a court case *"cannot be recreated simply within the datacenter."*
- **For the owner:** this is a well-known interviewer committing to a dated forecast with a stated probability, which makes it checkable. The 2028 median and the 25% tail are the numbers to hold him to. [Judgment.]
