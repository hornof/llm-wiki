## Summary

Latent Space podcast, 2026-11-02, 67 minutes, with Jacob Lauritzen, CTO of the legal-AI company Legora. Legora can't read its agent's traces, because the data belongs to law firms' clients. So it tests in simulated customer environments built by engineers and lawyers together. This page uses the episode notes and chapter list only; the transcript wasn't read. A companion Latent Space article, *Building AI for Lawyers*, was fetched but not used.

## Key Claims / Takeaways

- **The constraint:** *"Legora can’t read a single trace of what its agent does for a lawyer. The data belongs to the law firm’s clients."*
- **Testing without traces:** *"three engineers and three lawyers build test environments modeled on customers’ work, much like the “worlds” used in reinforcement learning"*, including a 10,000-file data room.
- **Model capability:** Lauritzen estimates *"that models already do about 60% of legal work well"*, and says the rest needs a smarter model.
- **Evals and model releases:** *"A citation problem where the model got it right 70% of the time, then 99% after one release. Legora didn’t have to change anything."*
- **Headcount:** a slide arguing 20 engineers would be enough forever; *"Now there are 100, and he still feels short-handed."* Legora also has 100 legal engineers, and the episode covers what legal engineers and forward-deployed engineers each do.
- **Model taste:** he finds Fable and Opus *"too chatty for drafting"* and prefers Astra's concise writing.
