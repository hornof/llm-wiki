## Summary

Gizmodo (Ece Yildirim, 2026-10-26) summarising a Washington Post exclusive (Elizabeth Dwoskin, [WaPo](https://www.washingtonpost.com/technology/2026/10/26/researchers-cybersecurity-start-up-cathedral-got-inside-anthropic/)) built on a draft report from Cathedral, a security startup founded by former DOGE staff. Cathedral's AI agents got into some of Anthropic's internal systems through flaws in third-party business software, during Anthropic's bug-bounty program. **Anthropic confirms limited access and disputes the rest.** The Post blocked a direct fetch, so Gizmodo's text is the source here, with the Digg page (https://digg.com/tech/dmkqyekn) for reporter posts.

## Key Claims / Takeaways

- **Cathedral's claim:** its agents *"took less than 12 hours to find paths to sensitive information, including meeting transcripts, salaries and job offers, and Slack messages."*
- **The way in was other companies' software:** the agents *"found ways into the company’s internal systems by exploiting vulnerabilities in third-party business software."* Anthropic says the team had *"previously spent several days unsuccessfully trying to breach its software directly."*
- **Anthropic's account:** access to *"a “limited set” of its sales, human resources, and IT systems"*, but not *"Claude users’ conversations, source code, or AI model weights."* It says Cathedral broke the program's rules *"by failing to disclose the full extent of its activity."*
- **Who Cathedral is:** founded by Gavin Kliger, Luke Farritor, Marko Elez and Jack Stein. Kliger was the Pentagon's chief data officer during its dispute with Anthropic. Valued at $1.4B after raising $160M (Gizmodo: *"valued at $1.4 billion and raised $160 million"*), with a16z and Sequoia partners on its board.
- **Politics:** an unnamed senior administration official told the Post the report *"raised national security concerns"* (Gizmodo's paraphrase). Given the founders' history with Anthropic, treat both sides' framing with care.
- **Not in the source:** when the access happened, or which vendors' software was the way in.
