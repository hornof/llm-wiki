## Summary

An amplification post — *"Jack Dorsey just open-sourced a toolkit for running a one-person company"* — pointing at `github.com/block/buzz`. **The post itself is stale**: [[buzz|Buzz]] was open-sourced on **2026-07-22**, two months earlier, and the wiki already records that.

**The replies are the value.** They are the first practitioner feedback on Buzz the wiki has captured, they arrive two months post-launch, and they are mixed-to-negative — which is exactly the interval at which the wiki usually has nothing, because it captures launches and rarely captures what happened next.

## Key Claims / Takeaways

### The pitch, as amplified

> *"It puts servers, automations, chat, search, Git, and permission-controlled AI teammates into one free, self-hosted setup."*

Consistent with the wiki's existing description — a self-hosted workspace where **people and agents are equal, cryptographically-identified members**.

### The replies — two months in

- **Jean-Christophe (@Jencryzthers)**, after two days of use: *"there is so many wierd glitches and the UX navigation for chat is giving me inception headaches. Will have to wait until it improve over time. Interesting project for sure!"* — interested, not retained.
- **0xbadhash**, after **two weeks**: *"Nice one but not mature unfortunately. Tested for 2 weeks then switched to Grok Bot, diff products but really better in term of alignment and responses."* The only reply reporting sustained use, and it ends in **churn to a competitor**.
- **DexorynLabs**: *"Self hosting needs steady power."* One line, and the real constraint on the whole self-hosted-workspace premise — the thing that makes it free is also the thing that makes it yours to keep running.
- **Mehdi Djabri**: *"He should add long-term agent memory"* — the gap a user notices first, and the one [[company-brain|the memory-substrate thread]] says nobody wants to own.

### What this is evidence of

**Not that Buzz failed** — three replies is not a sample, and two of the three are explicitly interested. What it evidences is that **two months after launch the visible practitioner experience is glitches, immaturity and one documented switch away**, with no counter-example in the thread reporting sustained use.

Worth holding against the architectural interest the wiki has recorded: Buzz is cited on several pages for its *design* (cryptographic identity, agents as first-class members, signed event log). **None of those pages carried any evidence that people are using it.** Now there is a little, and it is not encouraging.
