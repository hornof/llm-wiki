## Summary

TechCrunch (Rebecca Bellan) on Ramp card-spend data: business spending on AI tokens barely grew in August and September even though usage kept rising. Ramp's economist Ara Kharazian puts it down to price cuts, cheaper models and customers getting better at controlling spend, not weak demand. The article ties this to Anthropic's falling IPO valuation.

## Key Claims / Takeaways

- **Flat spend.** *"token spend only grew 1.5% across August and September"*, against July, when *"it grew 42% from the previous month"*. October picked up: *"token spend up 8.9% from September 23 to October 23"*.
- **Concentration risk.** *"Almost the entire spend on AI is from venture-backed, high-growth tech companies"*. The top 1% of firms came in *"just below the $10,000 per-employee"* of their summer peak. *"Median firms spend $12-$15 per employee per month, and the top 10% spend $600-$1,000"*.
- **Why.** Price cuts: *"OpenAI cut API prices in half for GPT-6 Sol and GPT-6 Luna"*, and Anthropic's Fable 5.1 and Opus 5.5 cuts *"slashed revenue growth by about $600 million"*, per a recent filing as TechCrunch reports it. Also thrift: experienced users set cheaper defaults, budgets and routers. Kharazian: *"You can use more AI, but use it for less."*
- **Jev named.** Kharazian says [[jev|Jev]] is *"increasingly among the top performers on Ramp"*: *"For a lot of AI use cases, you just need a simple Yes/No"*.
- **Valuations.** Bloomberg (as TechCrunch reports it): Anthropic's potential valuation fell *"from the $2 trillion estimated in early October to just $1.2 to $1.3 trillion"*. OpenAI lowered its proposed valuation *"to $1.25 trillion from $1.4 trillion"*.
- **Skeptical of the next wave.** Kharazian doubts personal agents, *"not as economically productive as coding"*, will restart growth.
- **Caveat.** Ramp sees its own customers' card and bill spend, which leans toward startups and mid-size firms. The article doesn't say how much of OpenAI's or Anthropic's revenue that covers.
