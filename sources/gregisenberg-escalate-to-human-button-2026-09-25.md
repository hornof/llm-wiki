## Summary

[[greg-isenberg|Greg Isenberg]] names a product gap in one paragraph: **every personal agent is fully AI, and none of them can hand a job to a person.**

> *"These apps handle almost everything, and then there's the **1 thing a week that needs an actual person**. Right now I'm the person. I'd pay real money for a button that hands the job to a human who finishes it and reports back."*

Short, but it carries a **checkable historical anchor and a falsifiable claim**, which is more than most product wishes.

## Key Claims / Takeaways

### The observation

> *"Every personal agent right now is fully AI. Muse, Instinct, all of them."*

### The precedent, and the number

> *"Meta tried this in 2015 with **M** and shut it down in 2018 because the human trainers ended up doing most of the work. **Humans did 90 and the AI did 10**, so every user cost a fortune."*

### The claim

> *"**That ratio is flipped now.**"*

**This is the whole argument, and it is falsifiable.** M failed on unit economics at 90/10 human/AI. If the ratio is now something like 10/90, the same product design becomes affordable — and nobody has shipped it. *(No evidence offered for the flip beyond the assertion.)*

> *"Instinct is worth $10B and it never hands anything to a person. I'd love to know what the version that actually finishes the job is worth!"*

## Why this lands on existing threads

**It is the missing half of a pattern the wiki already holds.** [[jev]]'s confidence-threshold design says: high confidence → act, medium → escalate to a stronger model, **low → route to a human**. The wiki recorded that as *the* practical pattern. Isenberg's point is that **the human-routing branch has no product behind it** — everyone specifies it and nobody operates it.

It also names the residue the [[meta|Muse]] story has been circling. Muse got a [[raw-batch-roundup-2026-09-25|rave NYT review]] and got [[amazon|blocked by Amazon]] in the same week, both over payment authority. Isenberg's *"1 thing a week that needs an actual person"* is a third framing of the same boundary: **not what the agent can do, but what happens at the edge of what it should.**

And it inverts the [[ai-native-service-companies|AI-native services]] thesis. Those firms sell **the outcome, delivered mostly by agents with a small human review layer**. Isenberg is asking for that layer **as a consumer button** — the same architecture, sold to an individual rather than a business.
