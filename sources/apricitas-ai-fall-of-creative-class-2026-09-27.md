## Summary

Joseph Politano's Apricitas Economics (2026-09-27; surfaced by `Daily Briefs/2026-10-24.md` four weeks late). US "creative" industries have lost over 200k jobs in four years, a stretch matched only by the 2001 and 2008 recessions, with no recession today. **Politano doesn't claim to measure AI's share.** He says it's *"extremely difficult"* to prove. His best evidence is a pattern: every digital-arts subsector is shedding jobs while in-person entertainment grows. He names rival causes, and his conclusion is that AI art matters more for culture than for the macroeconomy.

## Key Claims / Takeaways

- **The losses:**
  - Over 200k in four years ("more than 200k jobs in "creative" industries over the last four years, with roughly 50k lost within the last year alone").
  - Movie and TV is down over 100k, "nearly one-third of the sector's total."
  - Written publishing is down "more than 70k".
- **Why AI is hard to isolate (his words):** "Media firms are decidedly coy about their AI use". Other causes he lists: "consolidation in Hollywood, offshoring of content production, or the continued displacement of traditional media providers in favor of social media creators."
- **The evidence he does offer:** "all subsectors of the digital arts industry are losing jobs, while in-person entertainment is still growing at a healthy pace."
- **Streaming was already squeezing video before AI:** movie tickets were "down 25% from their 2019 peak" and cable revenue was "down 18% from its peak" (not inflation-adjusted).
- **AI use:** "Roughly 32% of workers in the overall arts, entertainment, and recreation sector use Generative AI to some extent, which is less than the 62% average across all industries". *A reader comment notes the 62% series includes personal use; the work-only FRED series reads 42%. The author didn't reply in the visible thread.*
- **Beyond the arts:** tech is "the clearest place where AI-driven job loss has shown up outside the media business". Tech job gains were "nearly 300k in 2022," and the sector has shed jobs every year since.
- **The macro picture:** "AI-driven job displacement has been relatively minor outside of these few narrow sectors". Legal and engineering firms have been hiring.
- **A claim the wiki doesn't hold, unverified:** "OpenAI learned this lesson the hard way when it was forced to abandon its video generation platform Sora". The wiki has no record of Sora being shut down. This is an economics blog stating it in passing, with no link.
