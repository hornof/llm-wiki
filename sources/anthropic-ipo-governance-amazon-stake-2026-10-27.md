## Summary

Two TechCrunch pieces from 2026-10-27, both reading the S-1 ([[anthropic-s1-public-filing-2026-10-26]]). Tim Fernholz lays out who controls the board after the IPO. Marina Temkin ([second article](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/27/vcs-will-make-billions-on-anthropics-ipo-but-amazon-is-set-to-make-much-much-more/)) argues that Amazon, not the VCs, is the biggest financial winner. **I checked two of Temkin's S-1 figures against the filing text: the $6.2B of debt and the 12% conversion cap. Both match.** Her other figures come from Amazon's filings, Bloomberg, the NYT and PitchBook, and weren't checked.

## Key Claims / Takeaways

- **Four layers of control (Fernholz):**
  - Anthropic is a public benefit corporation.
  - The Long-Term Benefit Trust can *"elect and remove a majority of the company’s board"* (Class T directors). Its chair is Neil Shah, and its members include Ben Bernanke, Richard Fontaine and Christopher Stubbs.
  - The seven founders jointly hold *"50.1% of shareholder voting power"*, which lets them elect three directors.
  - The board can block the Trust's moves on one "Specified" director and send the decision to an employee vote.
- **Fernholz's read:** public shareholders get *"little say over most major decisions"*, and the employee vote *"seems to formalize"* the employee power that reversed OpenAI's 2023 board action.
- **When it unwinds:** founders' control can end *"Ten years after the IPO"*, or automatically *"when fewer than three of them remain"*. The Trust then lasts until *"85% of common shareholders vote to end it"*.
- **No VC holds 5% (Temkin):** *"none of those VC firms owns 5%"*. At the private valuation, Menlo's position is *"only about 1.5% of the company"* (Bloomberg figures).
- **Amazon's notes:**
  - *"$6.2 billion in principal outstanding as of June 30"*, which is *"Anthropic’s entire debt load"*. The S-1 reads *"our total indebtedness was $6.2 billion"*.
  - Amazon's own filings value the notes at *"$97.9 billion as of June 30"*. That increase is the source of the $72.4B fair-value charge on the S-1 page.
  - Conversion is capped at 12% of the share count. The S-1 says *"Amazon would hold more than 12% of our capitalization"* if uncapped, and Amazon can waive the cap.
- **Amazon's total investment:** *"about $18 billion"*, including *"$10 billion in nonvoting preferred stock"*. Anthropic has committed to spend *"at least $110 billion on AWS over the next 10 years"*.
- **Google:** about 14% *"as of early last year"* per court filings. PitchBook estimates its current stake *"at closer to 5.6%"*. Google has *"at least $114 billion in cloud commitments"* through 2033.
- **The brief's draft** asks whether public-market discipline *"fixes the unit economics"*. Neither article argues that, and the S-1 already shows a profitable Q2 ([[anthropic-s1-public-filing-2026-10-26]]).
