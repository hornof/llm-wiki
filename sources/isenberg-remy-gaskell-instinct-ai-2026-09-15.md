## Summary

[[greg-isenberg|Greg Isenberg]] with **Remy Gaskell** of [[instinct|Instinct]], 28 minutes, published **2026-09-15**. The founder interview behind a product the wiki has referenced four times without holding a page for it.

**Listing depth only. No transcript obtained** — folded from the published episode description.

## Key Claims / Takeaways

- **The product:** a personal agent that lives **inside iMessage**. No app, no separate surface.
- **What it does:** books haircuts, makes restaurant reservations, handles **visa applications**, signs up for loyalty programmes.
- **How spending is controlled: spend-limited virtual cards.** The agent is given a card with a ceiling rather than an account.
- **Where it fails:** tasks that need **phone apps**, and **complex checkout pages**.
- **A privacy concern, named in the description:** **copies of email are retained after disconnection.**
- **Growth theory:** a trusted-person network, which the description frames as a potential network effect.

## Judgment

**The virtual-card mechanism is the most useful thing here** and it answers a question the wiki has been holding open. The agent-payment-authority thread has been a set of positions without a mechanism; *"give the agent a card with a ceiling, not an account"* is a concrete, boring, auditable answer — the budget is the blast radius. It sits between Muse-plus-Stripe delegation and outright blocking.

**The failure boundary is the honest part.** Phone apps and complex checkout pages is another way of saying *the agent works where there is a web surface it can drive and a form simple enough to complete* — the same wall every browser-driving agent hits. That the founder's own framing concedes it is worth more than the capability list.

**The retained-email-copies item is a real concern and should not be softened.** An agent with mailbox access that keeps copies after the user disconnects it has inverted the revocation model: disconnecting stops future reads, not past retention. The description names it; the wiki has no statement from Instinct on retention periods.

**Against [[greg-isenberg|Isenberg's own]] critique ten days later** ([[gregisenberg-escalate-to-human-button-2026-09-25]]): *"Instinct is worth $10B and it never hands anything to a person."* He interviewed the founder on 09-15 and named the missing escalation path on 09-25. Both positions are his, and the second is the sharper one.
