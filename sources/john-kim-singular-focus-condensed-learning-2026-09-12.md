## Summary

[[john-kim|John Kim]] (@PremiumGoblin, L7 at Meta) on the leverage of one defining goal, drawing on his own career switch — he quit a PM job at a hardware company at ~28, *"put my entire life on hold,"* and spent every waking hour learning to be a software engineer. Career-ramp content rather than a technical take; captured because Kim is a tracked practitioner voice and the argument is directly relevant to a compressed self-directed ramp.

## Key Claims / Takeaways

- **The mechanism**: *"No need to make plans, no distractions, no decision fatigue. What would have taken you years can really be condensed down to months."*
- **The 360-hour arithmetic**: a course is ~3 hours of lecture × ~15 weeks ≈ **45 hours**; the 8–10 core engineering courses that carry real working knowledge total **~360 hours of lecture time**. *"It's not a lot."* The rest of a degree is *"nice to know or if you want to specialize."*
- **His own hedge, in the post**: *"Yeah I know just listening to lectures is not the same as understanding, but the point is that if you are focused you can get to your goal fast."* The claim is about **scope**, not about lectures substituting for practice.
- **Counter in replies** — @nakularora: *"I find the notion of singular focus to be a bit unrealistic. You will never have 1 thing in life. The better approach is knowing how to balance the different priorities while allocating majority time/thought on the top 1-2 things."*
- Asked whether one should still write and review code manually rather than *"experimenting 100% with the latest tools,"* Kim declined a one-liner: *"My thoughts are nuanced on reviewing code."* Consistent with his standing position that **code review is his personal bottleneck** and that he still reads every line ([[john-kim-50-claude-code-tips-2026]]).
