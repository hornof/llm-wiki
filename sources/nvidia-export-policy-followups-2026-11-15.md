## Summary

Three Digg summaries from the 2026-11-15 brief on the policy fallout from Jensen Huang's exit at [[nvidia]], plus one TechCrunch piece. Digg's summaries cite the Financial Times, Bloomberg, The New York Times via Seeking Alpha, and Mint; none of those originals were fetched. Other Digg URLs: https://digg.com/tech/e767w1pk (Bessent), https://digg.com/tech/n6kv35ca (Lutnick). TechCrunch: https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/14/where-was-nvidias-security-team/.

## Key Claims / Takeaways

- **Congress wants a say over chip sales** (Digg, citing the FT). Bipartisan proposals *"would let Congress review and potentially block administration-approved sales"*, and a separate bill, the Remote Access Security Act, *"would stop Chinese companies from remotely accessing chips overseas through cloud services"*. They may ride on the defense authorization bill, but *"passage this year remains uncertain."* Huang has reportedly agreed to appear before Congress by Thanksgiving.
- **Treasury Secretary Bessent** (Digg, citing Bloomberg) called for a *"dramatic shakeup"* at Nvidia and suggested hiring from outside. Digg's note: *"It’s hiring advice, not an announced succession plan."*
- **Commerce Secretary Lutnick** (Digg, citing the NYT via Seeking Alpha) reportedly favors looser controls on overseas access to advanced chips, which could benefit data-center companies tied to Cantor Fitzgerald, now run by his sons. Digg adds: *"That business overlap alone doesn’t establish improper influence."*
- **TechCrunch asks what Nvidia's security team knew** about the Megaspeed inquiry. *"Nothing public connects"* the security chief's August departure to the matter, and the piece adds no new facts. Not recorded further here.
- **Why it matters here:** the cloud-access bill would reach the neocloud route that the [[depue-checkpoint-forensics-blackwell-2026-11-12|fingerprint report]] can't rule out, since it doesn't say where the chips were. [Judgment.]
