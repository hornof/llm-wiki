## Summary

TechCrunch (2026-10-20) on a draft federal bill that Anthropic shared with Politico, which reported it on 2026-10-16. Evaluators registered with the Commerce Department would assess models for catastrophic risk. If they find one, Commerce would ask a Washington, D.C. judge to halt the release. An Anthropic spokesperson confirmed the draft and said it reflects the company's Advanced AI Framework, which it has pitched to *"federal and state lawmakers since June."*

## Key Claims / Takeaways

- **Threshold:** a *"foreseeable, imminent risk"* that could kill more than 50 people or cause more than $1 billion in property damage. It must come from a biological weapon, *"the model evading its developer's controls"*, or a cyberattack.
- **Who assesses:** independent evaluators registered with Commerce. Labs may pay them as long as *"payment is not contingent on the evaluations' results."* TechCrunch says it is unclear how that would stop payment from shaping results.
- **Process:** evaluators have 72 hours to tell Commerce. Commerce asks a D.C. judge to stop the release.
- **The limit it sets:** per Politico, the bill would *"expressly forbid"* any federal agency from halting a model's distribution on its own authority. Only a court could.
- **Scope:** which models are covered is not given.
- **Context:** TechCrunch compares the thresholds to California's SB 53. It notes [[demis-hassabis|Hassabis]]'s FINRA-style self-governing body (2026-07-14) and Jensen Huang saying no new AI laws are needed (2026-09-15). It also says the bill's scenarios are *"a little bit far afield"* from the current main concern, agentic hacking.
