## Summary

Digg's summary (2026-10-27) of Reuters and Wall Street Journal reporting. A new special-purpose vehicle will borrow from banks and Blackstone, buy Broadcom AI chips and networking gear, and lease them to Anthropic, with deliveries from 2027. **The brief's headline calls this "Amazon's $63B Broadcom financing deal". Amazon isn't in the story.** The lenders are Bank of America, Citigroup, Morgan Stanley and Blackstone. Reuters and the WSJ were not fetched directly.

## Key Claims / Takeaways

- **Structure:** the banks *"will provide $63 billion in financing to buy Broadcom AI chips and networking gear for Anthropic"*. A *"newly formed special purpose vehicle will buy the equipment and lease it to Anthropic"*. Deliveries are expected *"to begin in 2027"*.
- **Tranches:** BofA, Citi and Morgan Stanley arrange the senior debt, and Blackstone provides the committed second-lien portion. Reuters put the senior portion at *"about $43.5 billion"*, citing two people. The companies' release gave no breakdown.
- **Broadcom is backstopping it:** Reuters says Broadcom gives senior lenders *"residual value support"*. Per the WSJ, *"some banks considered pulling out"*, and Hock Tan *"agreed to higher interest rates"* to keep them in.
- **Same shape as SpaceX's Apollo lease the day before** ([[spacex-apollo-40b-gpu-lease-2026-10-26]]): a financier owns the chips and the lab pays rent. Anthropic's lease payments are what repay the loans.
