## Summary

Anthropic's official position on open-weight models, written by [[dario-amodei|Dario Amodei]] — its direct response to the [[open-weights-american-ai-leadership-coalition-2026-07-24|25-signatory "Open Weights and American AI Leadership" coalition letter]]. The stance is a **nuanced middle**: Anthropic *"has never advocated for a ban on open-weights models"* (models without dangerous capabilities are *"a public good"*), but Amodei **disagrees that openness is inherently a safety benefit** and reframes his core worry as **geopolitical, not open-vs-closed**. *(Primary fetched.)*

## Key Claims / Takeaways

- **Not opposed to open weights**: *"Anthropic has never advocated for a ban on open-weights models"*; *"open-weights models that don't have dangerous capabilities are a public good."* Rejects *"protectionist bans"* as ineffective — *"a blanket ban on open-weights models is neither the correct remedy."*
- **Two distinct concerns**:
  - **Primary — geopolitical** (independent of open/closed): authoritarian governments building more powerful AI than the US for military superiority or repression.
  - **Secondary — safety/misuse**: open weights carry higher cyber/bio misuse risk because *"it is very difficult to apply guardrails"* and weights can't be withdrawn after release — but a ban wouldn't fix this since *"bad actors are unlikely to be legitimate US businesses."*
- **Three targeted measures** (vs. blanket bans): (1) **chip controls** — *"we should not sell powerful chips or chipmaking equipment to China"* + crack down on smuggling; (2) **distillation crackdown** on *"industrial-scale distillation operations"* that let China build better models with fewer chips; (3) **mandatory safety testing** — *"all sufficiently capable models, open and closed, should go through mandatory safety testing."*
- **Explicit disagreement with the coalition**: agrees on expanding access + strengthening competition, but **rejects the letter's claim that open weights necessarily improve safeguards** — argues risk should be *determined empirically by testing*, not assumed (openness ≠ automatically helps defenders more than attackers). Directly counters the coalition's *"openness-as-safety"* framing.

## Why it matters

- **Completes the open-weights governance cluster**: [[us-treasury-china-ai-sanctions-threat-2026-07-21|Bessent sanctions threat]] → [[open-weights-american-ai-leadership-coalition-2026-07-24|coalition rebuttal]] → **Anthropic's middle position** — chip/distillation controls + mandatory testing, but no blanket ban. Notably, Anthropic's *distillation crackdown* stance **partly sides with Bessent** against the coalition's "distillation is legitimate" line, while its *no-ban* stance sides with the coalition.
- A rare case of a frontier lab publicly splitting the difference in a live policy fight.
