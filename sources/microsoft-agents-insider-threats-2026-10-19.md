## Summary

TechCrunch (Julie Bort, 2026-10-19) on Microsoft's agent-security launch, **Guardian Agents**, part of the Project Perception security suite introduced in June. Microsoft's security chief Hayete Gallot writes that *"frontier models, regardless of their origin, cannot be trusted."* The article also quotes a private blog post from [[satya-nadella|Nadella]] earlier in October arguing that enterprises need monitoring and control systems that the AI labs don't run. TechCrunch calls the position "surprising and self-serving."

## Key Claims / Takeaways

- **Gallot's reason for distrust:** frontier models are not malicious or easily hacked, but unpredictable. They are trained to complete tasks, not to follow "organizational norms or judgment," and are often given access to data and tools that can do real harm.
- **Nadella (private blog post, October):** *"we simply can't outsource responsibility for what intelligence does on our behalf"* and *"we need to separate the supply of intelligence from the authority over it."*
- **Controls announced:**
  - Guardian Agents monitor the agents a company uses.
  - Microsoft Defender detects up to 60 agent types employees bring in, naming Claude Code, Codex and OpenClaw.
  - Agent access to data and systems is restricted, with permissions adjusted to the task.
  - A human has to approve actions that look risky.
- **Partners and hardware:** Microsoft is an inaugural member of NVIDIA's Open Agent Safety Platform (100+ companies), which isolates agents and can stop them at the hardware level. Surface Laptop Ultra PCs ship with agent controls.
- **Project Perception** originally had red, blue and green team agents aimed at outside threats such as hacking with Anthropic's models and OpenAI's Daybreak scanner.
- **No incident is cited.** TechCrunch notes that Microsoft has invested in both OpenAI and Anthropic and that OpenAI is one of its largest cloud customers.
