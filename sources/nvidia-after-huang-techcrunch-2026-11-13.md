## Summary

Three TechCrunch pieces and one Digg summary on what [[jensen-huang|Jensen Huang]]'s exit from [[nvidia|Nvidia]] means, published 2026-11-13 and 11-14:

- Tim Fernholz on Will DePue's weight-fingerprint analysis of Chinese open models (the URL above).
- Marina Temkin and Dominic-Madori Davis on Nvidia as a startup investor: https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/13/jensen-huangs-departure-blasts-big-hole-in-ai-startup-funding-vcs-and-founders-say/
- Kate Park on reactions in Singapore, South Korea, Taiwan and Japan: https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/14/asian-governments-react-to-nvidia-ceo-jensen-huangs-exit/
- Digg on [[nathan-lambert|Nathan Lambert]]'s Interconnects essay "Open-source AI without Jensen": https://digg.com/tech/mfak7vp3

TechCrunch now names the executive at the center of the reports. As on [[nvidia-ceo-transition-2026-11-12]], this page names her company, Megaspeed, and not her.

## Key Claims / Takeaways

- **Fingerprints (DePue, via TechCrunch):** *"the most closely watched models, including Moonshot’s Kimi’s K3 releases and DeepSeek’s V4 models, bear fingerprints from training on Blackwell chips."* TechCrunch's caveat: *"That doesn’t mean that these models were trained entirely on Nvidia chips, or that export control laws were necessarily violated"*, since the chips could sit in an offshore data center.
- **Scale estimates:** Epoch AI *"estimates that about two million advanced chips entered China in 2024 and 2025 through schemes designed to hide their final destination"* and *"275,000 Nvidia AI accelerators were imported to Malaysia in 2024 and 2025."* *"Some 600,000 H200 chips are reportedly stockpiled in Hong Kong"*.
- **Nvidia's exposure:** *"Nvidia’s potential corporate liability for export control violations likely factored into its decision to part ways with Huang."* TechCrunch reports that the US and Singapore governments have both investigated Megaspeed.
- **Nvidia as investor:** *"participating in 105 rounds this year alone, often as the largest investor, according to PitchBook."* One VC: *"Nvidia is carrying almost every AI-native startup from application to neocloud"*. On Kress: *"She’s much more conservative,"*. Infrastructure startups needing *"$100 million to $500 million in GPUs would often raise half of that amount at valuations of $1 billion or more from Nvidia just to get started"*.
- **Who fills the gap:** Battery Ventures' Dharmesh Thakker expects Big Tech to compete for the role but doubts they will fund pre-revenue infrastructure startups the way Nvidia did. *"When Nvidia was on the cap table, everyone felt safe,"* said Seligman Ventures' Umesh Padval.
- **Asia:** South Korea's Science Ministry said Nvidia *"had reaffirmed its plan to supply 260,000 advanced GPUs to the country"*. Taipei said Nvidia's planned overseas headquarters *"remained on track"*. Reuters counted *"more than $3 billion in Chinese AI computing projects reliant on Nvidia’s restricted processors"* now in doubt.
- **Lambert on open models:** the funding Huang drove *"will very likely dry up."* *"My biggest near-term worry is sloppy regulation at the state level, followed by national regulation,"*. He calls for a US open-model lab and *"soul-searching and coalition building"*. Digg: *"His essay identifies no announced post-departure funding cuts."*
- **Why it matters here:** the neoclouds in [[ai-margin-collapse]] were financed partly on Nvidia's checks and guarantees. If Kress is more conservative, that's the funding source most likely to tighten first. [Judgment.]
