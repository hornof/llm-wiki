## Summary

Divergence Labs' preprint (PDF dated 2026-10-27) on an agentic antibody-design campaign with Claude. The target is an antibody that physically removes IgE from its receptor on mast cells, which the only approved food-allergy biologic, omalizumab, doesn't do well. It's the primary source behind the Divergence case in Anthropic's 10-27 drug-development post ([[anthropic-drug-development-early-progress-2026-10-27]]). The results are lab assays, not animal or human data.

## Key Claims / Takeaways

- **Hit rate:** *"Of 91 experimentally characterized disruptor designs, 20 showed activity against receptor-bound IgE"*.
- **Against the approved drug:** *"three variants removed more IgE than omalizumab at therapeutically relevant concentrations (25–400 nM)"*.
- **Anchor arm:** de novo designs bound IgE *"with apparent affinities of 3.4–235 nM"*.
- **How the work was split:** humans guided, and Claude decomposed the objective and chose the redesign phases. For example, *"Claude then elected to split subsequent disruptor redesign efforts into six distinct sub-phases"*.
- **Speed claim:** results *"obtained within weeks rather than the months expected of traditional screening workflows"*.
- **Stage:** the parts are *"the starting point for further in vitro and in vivo characterization"*. No combined molecule has been tested yet.
