## Summary

Digg summary (2026-10-19) of a Wall Street Journal report on a DHS notice, also reported by RuntimeWire. Federal civilian agencies must remove [[deepseek|DeepSeek]] technology from their IT systems and may not buy DeepSeek products. The ban also covers DeepSeek affiliates and third-party products built on DeepSeek. **The order is dated 2026-07-24** and became public when it was filed for public inspection on 10-19, with Federal Register publication set for 10-20.

## Key Claims / Takeaways

- **Scope:** removal from federal civilian systems; a ban on future purchases; covers affiliates and **third-party products built on DeepSeek implementations.** Consumers are not covered.
- **Timing:** dated 2026-07-24, public 2026-10-19. Agencies had a 60-day window to request exceptions, which had already closed before the order was public.
  > **2026-10-20 — contested.** TechCrunch gives the order date as 2026-10-20, the day it was published in the Federal Register (2026-21447), and says agencies *can* request exceptions. The Federal Register metadata has no signing date. The 07-24 date rests on RuntimeWire via Digg, so treat it as unconfirmed.
  > **2026-10-21 — settled.** The signed order in the Federal Register appendix (pp. 66347–66350) reads *"Original Release Date: July 24, 2026"*, signed by Secretary Markwayne Mullin, and sets a 60-day exception window from issuance. **07-24 is the order date; 10-20 is the publication date.** The original reading stands, with one limit: the order ran nearly three months before Federal Register publication, but whether anyone reported it earlier is unknown.
- **Identifiers:** RuntimeWire cites "Order #2026-01"; the Federal Register public-inspection document is 2026-21447.
- **No compliance deadline** is given in the summary.
